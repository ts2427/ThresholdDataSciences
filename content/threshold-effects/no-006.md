On Monday I made an argument: rules change what organizations are required
to do, but rarely change what they are. The research I cited backs it. So does
most of what I've seen in 20 years of operations.

But the honest version of that argument has to survive its best
counterexample — and I'd rather go looking for it than wait for it to find
me.

So here's the question: what's the strongest case you've seen of a rule
that actually changed what organizations are? Not compliance on paper — real,
durable change in how firms behave, staff, invest, or think. A regulation that
built capacity rather than just revealing who already had it.

Seatbelt-style engineering mandates? SOX internal controls, a decade on?
Something in your industry I've never heard of?

I have a candidate or two of my own, but I'm more interested in yours.

Best counterexample gets a genuine rethink in a future post.
